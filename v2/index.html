Base campMonths 1–3

In your first four weeks, a stranger pays you for something you made.

It's a small first step, and you can check it. Everything after builds on it. The business school where the grade is a stranger's payment.

Missions, matched to where you are.

There are no modules to sit through. Each week you get missions for your stage. You do them in the real world, then bring back proof: a short video with your reflection in it, and an entry in the sales log whenever a stranger pays. Finish the core missions at one level and the next one opens.

The missions come from how bootstrapped founders got started: talking to customers before building, making an offer people want, and asking for money before you feel ready.

Level 0 Get used to talking to strangers and naming a price.

  • W2Warm up45 min

    Something of value

    Offering something to a stranger and naming a price

    The brief

    Go outside and offer something of value, an item or a service, to someone you don't know, for £1. Want the bonus? Charge £10 instead. Be honest about what it is and accept a no.

    Bring back

    A clip of your best offer and a tally of ten asks: paid or no.

  • W3Warm up20 min

    Discount coffee

    Asking for a better price, politely, and hearing no without backing down

    The brief

    Go to a coffee shop, order a coffee and ask for a discount. Be polite, give a reason if you have one, and take no for an answer.

    Bring back

    A short video, filmed with the staff's permission or outside straight after.

Level 1 Find people who already spend time or money on a problem.

  • C1Customers

    Problem hunt

    Finding a problem people already spend time or money on, without pitching

    The brief

    Find 10 people who have the problem. Don't pitch anything. Ask how they solve it today and what frustrates them about what they use now. No idea yet? Pick a group of people and ask what annoyed them most this week, and what they last paid to fix.

    Bring back

    Your notes from ten conversations and a short reflection on camera.

    Uses Rob Fitzpatrick's Mom Test

  • J1Judgement30 min

    Set your line

    Setting a stop rule before you see the results

    The brief

    Before you ask for money, decide what would make you drop or change this idea. Set your line: the fewest yeses out of ten that keeps the idea alive. A well-reasoned low line scores the same as a high one.

    Bring back

    A filmed call explaining your line.

Level 2 Put the whole business on one card, then make the offer worth buying.

  • C3Customers

    £100M Offer

    Making an offer people want: bigger outcome, more belief, less time and effort

    The brief

    Write your offer using Alex Hormozi's value equation: the dream outcome for your customer, why they'll believe it works, how fast they get it and how little effort it takes. Add the price.

    Bring back

    Your written offer, with the price on it.

    Adapted from Alex Hormozi's $100M Offers

  • C2Customers

    The one minute model

    Putting the whole business on one card: who pays, for what, and what you keep

    The brief

    Fill in one card: who your customer is, their problem, what you sell, the price, what it costs you to deliver and what you keep. Want the bonus? Explain it on camera in under a minute.

    Bring back

    A photo of the card.

  • Next: Ask for moneyOpens when you finish this level’s core missions.

Level 3 Ask ten people to pay. Learn from every no.

  • C4Customers

    10 asks

    Asking people for money and learning from every no

    The brief

    Find ten people in your customer segment and ask them to pre-order or buy. Before you start, guess how many will say yes. Ask the ones who say no what would change their mind.

    Bring back

    Your guess, a tally of ten and a clip of your best ask.

  • S2Sales

    First paying customer

    Closing a sale with someone new

    The brief

    Get one person you didn't know before today to pay for your thing. Money in hand or a paid pre-order. It can be a product, a service you can do on the spot, or a pre-order.

    Bring back

    The receipt, who they are and how you found them. It goes in the sales log.

  • B1Building with AI60 min

    The landing page

    Using AI to build only what a customer needs to say yes

    The brief

    Build a simple landing page for your offer with an AI app builder. It needs who it's for, the problem, the price and one sign-up or pay button. Say one change you made to what the AI built.

    Bring back

    A short walk-through video. Bonus if someone outside your team signs up or pays on it.

Level 4 Change the offer because of what customers said, not what you hoped.

  • C5Customers

    Improve the offer

    Changing your offer because of what customers said, not what you hoped

    The brief

    Look at what people told you in your ten asks. Change the price, who it's for, what's included or how you say it.

    Bring back

    A photo of the new offer, and on camera: what you changed and why.

  • D3Customers

    Three dated yeses

    Turning interest into commitments with a date

    The brief

    Ask up to ten people for a commitment with a date: a paid pre-order, a call booked in the next seven days, or an introduction to someone who would pay. You need three. 'Sounds good' doesn't count.

    Bring back

    Three commitments, each with a date.

Level 5 Full price, more strangers, bigger deals with clear terms.

  • S3Sales

    Three sales in one run

    Closing several strangers at full price in one run

    The brief

    Get three people you didn't know before today to pay your full price, at least £10, in one run. Ask up to twenty.

    Bring back

    Three entries in the sales log, with proof of payment.

  • P3Sales

    The £50 deal

    Agreeing a bigger deal with clear terms

    The brief

    Get one person or business you didn't know before to pay £50 or more, or a £50 deposit, for something you'll deliver within seven days. Agree in writing what they get, when, and the refund if you don't deliver.

    Bring back

    The written terms and proof of payment.

  • F1Flash missionAll weekend

    Paperclip challenge

    Trading up: making each swap worth more to the other person

    The brief

    Take a paperclip and trade it up. Swap it for something worth more, then swap that. Log each trade. Finish with the most valuable thing you can.

    Bring back

    A clip of each trade and a photo of what you got.

These are real missions from our 48h Startup Challenge. In the programme they get longer and harder as you level up.

The recipe

  1. Do the mission for real. You work with real people, in London or online. It isn't a case study.
  2. Film the proof. A short video with your reflection inside it: what you guessed, what happened, what you'd change.
  3. Log every sale. Proof of payment from someone you didn't already know. That's the grade.
Camp IMonths 4–6

Twelve months, four camps.

Each stage takes about three months. Pick a camp to see what you do there and what you leave with.

Months 1–3 · Base camp

Find your fit. Make a first sale.

The first missions cover founder-idea fit. You work out which kind of business you're best placed to build. You talk to people about problems they already pay to fix, and you make small test offers to strangers while you decide.

You leave this stage withTen problem conversations, your first test offers and your first stranger payment in the sales log.

Missions like

  • The opener
  • Problem hunt
  • Something of value
  • Set your line

Months 4–6 · Camp I

Shape an offer and sell it.

You turn what you heard into an offer people want, test three prices and ask for money again and again. You build with AI only what helps a customer say yes: a landing page, a demo, a first version.

You leave this stage withAn offer strangers pay for, a page that sells it and a sales log that keeps growing.

Missions like

  • £100M Offer
  • Three prices
  • 10 asks
  • The landing page

Months 7–9 · Camp II

Find one channel that works.

One channel at a time, until it works. You use AI for outreach, lead lists and a weekly content system, and you hand the repetitive parts of the business to AI agents. You learn to read your own numbers.

You leave this stage withA repeatable way to find customers, and unit economics that tell you whether it pays.

Missions like

  • Twenty messages
  • Lead generation
  • Content system
  • Ask for an intro

Months 10–12 · Summit

Make it profitable. Make it last.

Bigger deals, firmer prices and better delivery. You negotiate, run the business with a small crew of AI agents, and decide on the evidence what year two looks like.

You leave this stage withA business with verified revenue, clear margins and a plan for the next twelve months.

Missions like

  • The £50 deal
  • Double the price
  • Three sales in one run
  • The next 7 days

Is it for you?

Questions, answered straight.

Real accelerators pay you. Why would I pay Fabrum?

Entrepreneur First and Antler are free or invest in you, and when they invest they take a share of your company. They're built for people ready to go full-time on a company now. That's a good deal if you're at that point. Fabrum is for the stage before, or alongside: learning to find buyers, build with AI and sell, before anyone has a reason to invest. Fabrum takes no equity. If you get into an accelerator afterwards, that's a good outcome for us too.

Is this a course where I learn AI?

No. You don't learn to build with AI by watching someone else do it. You use AI on real missions, and an AI build only counts when a real stranger responds to it. The tools will change every few months. Knowing how to turn them into something people pay for won't.

I want to build a product. Why sell first?

Because it's the fastest way to find out what's worth building. Selling something small to a stranger tells you more in a week than six months of building alone. With AI, building has become the quicker part. Finding the buyer is the skill most technical people skip.

Do I need a business idea to apply?

No. The first missions cover founder-idea fit, so you pick the kind of idea you're best suited to. Then you build it and sell it yourself. What we look for is drive and a willingness to go and talk to people.

Do I need to quit my job or leave my degree?

No. Cohort 1 is part-time and runs alongside work or study. It isn't a hobby, though. Expect about [hours] a week of missions, events and one-to-ones, every week for 12 months. If you can't protect that time, wait for a later cohort.

I'm a student. Is this for me?

Yes, if you already do things beyond your degree. Cohort 1 has room for students and for people a few years into work. The bar is the same: show us what you've done, not what you've studied.

How am I assessed?

On proof of what you did, never on exams. Each mission ends in a short proof video with your reflection in it. Sales count in full only with proof that a stranger paid.
